# Memo: Where to focus in England's home retrofit market

**Audience:** investment committee at a UK impact or infrastructure investor (illustrative).
**Date:** 15 October 2026

## Recommendation

Focus first on retrofit work in private rented homes ahead of the October 2030 landlord deadline, and treat owner-occupier retrofit as upside that needs a financing product to unlock, not as a base case to underwrite.

## Why

**The market is large but only partly funded.** 11.35m English homes sit below EPC band C. Upgrading all of them would cost about £66bn. In a base case where uptake follows the policy deadlines, spend is £34.4bn on 5.9m homes between 2026 and 2035, peaking at £6.05bn in 2030. The £15bn Warm Homes Plan covers about 44% of that, leaving roughly £19.4bn for private capital.

**Private rented demand is the most predictable.** Landlords face a 2030 deadline and a £10,000 cost cap, with government estimating an average cost of about £5,400. The base case puts this segment at £10.9bn, concentrated in 2026 to 2030, so deal flow arrives early.

**Owner-occupier demand is the biggest and least certain.** It is £14.8bn of the base case and drives the widest swing: total spend moves from £25.9bn to £45.0bn if uptake is 15% or 60%. No rule compels these households to act, so uptake depends on finance being available and affordable.

**Cost per home is the second risk.** A 30% move in the cost of upgrading a D-rated home shifts the total between £27.3bn and £41.5bn.

## What would change this view

- A confirmed social housing standard would add certainty to a further £8.6bn.
- Evidence that private rented homes sit well below the national EPC average would raise the private rented estimate.
- Measured regional cost data would replace the cost assumptions, which are the weakest inputs.

## Caveats

Costs for D-rated and E to G homes, uptake rates and the 2035 horizon are assumptions, not sourced figures. The £15bn includes loans and measures beyond EPC C works, so the funding comparison is indicative. See the README for the full list.
